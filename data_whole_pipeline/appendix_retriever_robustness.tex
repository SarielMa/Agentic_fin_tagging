\caption{Retriever robustness. This table matters more than usual here: the coverage term of
Eq.~\ref{eq:coverage} corrects a sparse-scoring length pathology that may not exist under dense
representations, so whether the architecture's gains persist without it is an open question.
The answer is that they largely do not. Under BM25, AGS improves R@10 by $+0.079$ over one-pass
($\dagger$: the only comparison in this table whose paired 95\% CI excludes zero,
Table~\ref{tab:retriever_robustness_deltas}). Under a dense retriever with the coverage term
removed, that gain disappears entirely: AGS and one-pass are separated by $+0.000$ at R@10 and
$-0.002$ at R@50. The gain returns only when the coverage term is switched back on
(rows marked \textsuperscript{+cov}), which places most of AGS's retrieval advantage in the
lexical coverage feature rather than in the hypothesis-generation architecture. Rows without
\textsuperscript{+cov} use $w_{\text{cov}}{=}0$ for dense and hybrid, since coverage is a lexical
feature; BM25 rows use the main-results setting $w_{\text{cov}}{=}1$. Pooled over
$n{=}2509$ facts from 191 source contexts (table and text). Dense index: \texttt{BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5},
exact cosine over unit-normalized embeddings, no ANN approximation. Hybrid: reciprocal rank
fusion of the BM25 and dense lists, $\kappa{=}60$. Bold marks the best value per column.
All rows replay the methods' own logged query strings verbatim -- no hypothesis is regenerated --
and consolidate through the identical frozen-AGS code path at $\beta{=}0.6$, which is
\emph{not} re-tuned per retriever.}
\label{tab:retriever_robustness}
